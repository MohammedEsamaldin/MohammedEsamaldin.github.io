About

I'm an AI safety researcher from Sudan, based in London. I build cheap monitors that read a model's internals to catch misalignment before it shows in behaviour. I focus on open-weight models because they are what low-resource communities can actually deploy, and those communities are where a misaligned model is most likely to go unnoticed.

I'm currently a BASE Fellow (AI Catastrophic Risk track), working with my mentor Sultan Hassan on whether optimisation geometry during fine-tuning can predict emergent misalignment. Alongside that, I'm building a testbed to attribute goal drift in LLM agents from their internal representations. I also helped start AI Safety Arabia, which explains AI safety risks to Arabic-speaking audiences.

Before moving into AI safety, I worked in industry as an ML engineer, data scientist and AI research engineer, and I completed an MSc in Artificial Intelligence (Distinction) at Queen Mary University of London as a Chevening Scholar.

I'm open to research collaborations and AI safety roles. The best way to reach me is by email.

Research

Can Optimisation Geometry Predict Emergent Misalignment?

BASE Fellowship, AI Catastrophic Risk track. Mentor: Sultan Hassan. October 2026 to present. In progress

Narrow fine-tuning can make a model broadly misaligned. We are reproducing this on small open-weight models, then tracking loss-landscape geometry across fine-tuning checkpoints to test whether it signals misalignment before behavioural evaluations do.

Forgotten, Inferred, or Substituted: Attributing Goal Drift in LLM Agents from Internal Representations

Independent research. 2026 to present. In progress

A testbed that produces three causes of goal drift in one agent environment (interference, an inherited drifted trajectory, and backdoored goal substitution), matched on behaviour, to test whether internal representations of Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-14B can tell the causes apart when behaviour cannot. Each research question has a kill condition stated in advance.

Proposal

Mechanistic Interpretability of Factual Recall in Pythia-1.4B

Independent research. 2026.

I used activation patching with TransformerLens to trace how subject information flows to the answer: it is picked up early, held at a post-subject position through the middle layers, and moved to the final token late.

ZANOBIA: A Socratic Framework for Bidirectional Detection and Mitigation of Hallucinations in Multimodal LLMs

MSc thesis (Distinction), Queen Mary University of London. 2025.

Detects and mitigates hallucinations from both sides: it clarifies ambiguous user prompts with Socratic questioning and checks model answers against external knowledge and visual evidence, with no retraining. On MMHal-Bench with LLaVA-7B, it lowered the hallucination rate from 0.76 to 0.64 (16% lower).

Defensive AI Governance for Fragile States: A Sovereignty-First Framework

Draft. 2024.

A framework for deploying AI in states without formal regulatory infrastructure, using Sudan as the main case study. It turns the EU AI Act, NIST and Singapore frameworks into seven principles enforced through soft-law levers such as procurement rules, audits and transparency labels.
